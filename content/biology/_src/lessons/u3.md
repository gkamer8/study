### Key terms

- **Annotation**: labels on raw sequence marking where genes, exons and other features are and what they do.
- **Accession**: a database's stable ID for a record (see Unit 2).
- **Reference genome**: the standard genome sequence that coordinates refer to, e.g. hg38 (see Unit 6).
- **Track**: one row of data drawn along genome coordinates in a browser.
- **Ortholog**: the same gene in another species (see Unit 2).
- **Paralog**: a related gene in the same species, from duplication (see Unit 2).
- **Expression**: how much RNA or protein a gene produces in a sample.
- **eQTL**: a genetic variant associated with how strongly a gene is expressed.
- **Allele frequency (AF)**: the fraction of chromosomes in a population carrying a particular variant version.
- **Exome sequencing**: sequencing only the protein-coding exons (~1–2% of the genome).
- **Whole-genome sequencing (WGS)**: sequencing all of an individual's DNA.
- **Mendelian disease**: a disease caused mainly by variants in a single gene.
- **Pathogenic**: clinical label meaning disease-causing.
- **Benign**: clinical label meaning harmless.
- **Loss-of-function (LoF)**: describes a variant that destroys the gene product (nonsense, frameshift, splice-site).
- **Constraint**: how strongly natural selection removes damaging variants from a gene.
- **X-ray crystallography**: determining a 3D structure from how a protein crystal diffracts radiation.
- **Cryo-EM**: determining a 3D structure by electron microscopy of flash-frozen samples.
- **NMR**: determining a 3D structure from magnetic resonance of molecules in solution.
- **Domain**: an independently folding unit of a protein (see Unit 4).
- **Pathway**: a set of molecules acting in sequence to carry out a process (see Unit 5).
- **Ontology**: a controlled vocabulary whose terms have defined relationships to each other.
- **Enrichment analysis**: testing whether a gene list contains more members of some category than chance predicts.
- **Cell line**: cells that grow indefinitely in the lab, often derived from a tumor.
- **Raw data**: the sequencer's original reads, before any processing.
- **Processed data**: tables derived from reads, such as gene counts or peak calls.

## The mental model: one question, one database

Most lookups answer one of a few questions: *what is this gene?*, *what does its protein look like?*, *where is it expressed?*, *which variants exist and do they matter?*, *what does it interact with?*, or *where is the raw data?* Each has a standard resource. Learn the mapping and you'll know what a paper means by "we downloaded X from Y".

## Genes and genomes

- **NCBI Gene** (NCBI = the US National Center for Biotechnology Information): one page per gene with a numeric ID (TP53 = 7157), a plain-language summary, aliases, its RefSeq transcripts, and links. Good first stop for *"what is this gene?"*.
- **Ensembl** (from EMBL-EBI, the European Bioinformatics Institute): genome **annotation** for hundreds of vertebrates. Annotation means the labels marking where genes, transcripts and exons are. Ensembl provides gene/transcript/protein models (ENSG/ENST/ENSP), **orthologs** and **paralogs**, the **VEP** (Variant Effect Predictor, which labels a variant as missense, splice, etc.), and **BioMart** for bulk ID conversion. **GENCODE** is the human and mouse gene annotation that Ensembl displays.
- **UCSC Genome Browser** (University of California, Santa Cruz): a visual browser organized by genome coordinates on a specific **assembly** (hg19 or hg38, versions of the reference genome; see Unit 6). Data are drawn as stacked **tracks**: genes, conservation across species, regulatory data, repeats, variants. Tools: the **Table Browser** (downloads) and **liftOver** (converting coordinates between assemblies). It answers *"what is at chr7:140,753,336?"*.
- **HGNC**: the authority on official human gene symbols, previous symbols and aliases (see Unit 2).

## Proteins and structures

- **UniProt** (UniProtKB): the reference for protein sequences and function, with one entry per protein. Each entry lists its function, **domains** (independently folding units; see Unit 4), modification sites, isoforms, disease variants, and links. **Swiss-Prot** entries are reviewed by curators; **TrEMBL** entries are automatic. **UniRef50/90/100** clusters are standard training sets for protein language models.
- **PDB** (Protein Data Bank; run by RCSB in the US, PDBe in Europe and PDBj in Japan, together wwPDB): experimentally determined 3D structures. They come from **X-ray crystallography** (X-rays diffracted by a protein crystal), **cryo-EM** (electron microscopy of flash-frozen samples) and **NMR** (magnetic resonance in solution). There are roughly two hundred thousand entries, each with a 4-character ID. It answers *"is there a measured structure, at what resolution, bound to what?"*. Resolution is in ångströms (Å, 0.1 nm), and lower is sharper.
- **AlphaFold DB** (Google DeepMind + EMBL-EBI): *predicted* structures for more than 200 million UniProt proteins, with per-residue confidence (pLDDT) and error plots (PAE); see Unit 4. IDs look like AF-P04637-F1.
- **InterPro / Pfam**: catalogs of protein families and domains. Pfam (PF00069 = protein kinase domain) is now browsed through the InterPro website. It answers *"what domains does this protein have?"*.

## Expression

**Expression** means how much RNA (or protein) a gene makes in a given sample.

- **GTEx** (Genotype-Tissue Expression): RNA sequencing of ~50 tissues from roughly 1,000 deceased donors, plus **eQTLs**. An eQTL is a genetic variant whose genotype correlates with a gene's expression level. GTEx answers *"in which normal tissues is this gene on, and which variants change its level?"*.
- **Human Protein Atlas**: protein and RNA levels by tissue, cell type and location within the cell, with antibody-staining images.
- **CELLxGENE** (Chan Zuckerberg Initiative): curated **single-cell** datasets, meaning expression measured cell by cell (see Unit 6). Its Census holds tens of millions of cells in one standard format. It is a common pretraining source for single-cell foundation models.
- **GEO** (Gene Expression Omnibus, NCBI): archive for **processed** functional-genomics data (expression tables, peak files) and sample descriptions. Accessions: **GSE** (series = a study), **GSM** (sample), **GPL** (platform = the instrument or array type), GDS (curated dataset).
- **SRA** (Sequence Read Archive, NCBI): **raw** sequencing reads. Accessions: **SRP** study, **SRX** experiment, **SRS** sample, **SRR** run (the actual files). European (ENA, ERR…) and Japanese (DDBJ, DRR…) archives mirror it. Related IDs: **BioProject** (PRJNA…) and **BioSample** (SAMN…).

## Variants and disease

- **dbSNP**: NCBI's catalog of short variants; issues rsIDs (see Unit 2).
- **gnomAD** (Genome Aggregation Database): **allele frequencies**, the fraction of chromosomes carrying each variant, from hundreds of thousands of **exomes** (only the exons sequenced) and **genomes** (v4 ≈ 800k people), broken down by genetic ancestry. It also reports **gene constraint**: whether a gene has fewer **loss-of-function** (LoF, gene-breaking) variants than expected, because selection removes them. Scores: pLI and **LOEUF** (loss-of-function observed/expected upper bound fraction; low = intolerant). It answers *"how common is this variant in the general population?"*. A variant carried by many healthy people is unlikely to cause a severe disease.
- **ClinVar**: clinical labs' interpretations of variants: **pathogenic** (disease-causing), likely pathogenic, **VUS** (variant of uncertain significance), likely benign, **benign**, with 0–4 review-status stars. Accessions: VCV (variant), RCV (variant + condition), SCV (one submission). It is the standard benchmark for variant-effect predictors.
- **OMIM** (Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man): curated catalog of **Mendelian** (single-gene) disorders and their genes, with 6-digit numbers. Prefixes: `*` gene, `#` disease with known molecular cause, `%` disease with unknown cause, `+` gene and phenotype combined.
- **COSMIC**, **cBioPortal**, **TCGA** (The Cancer Genome Atlas): somatic (tumor) mutations and multi-omic tumor cohorts.
- **GWAS Catalog**: published trait–variant associations from GWAS (see Unit 1).

## Function, pathways and interactions

- **Gene Ontology (GO)**: an **ontology** is a controlled vocabulary with relationships between its terms, here arranged as a directed acyclic graph from general to specific. GO has three aspects: **Molecular Function** (e.g. "kinase activity"), **Biological Process** (e.g. "apoptotic process", GO:0006915), and **Cellular Component** (e.g. "nucleus"). Genes are annotated to terms with **evidence codes**; IEA ("inferred from electronic annotation") is automatic and weakest. GO powers **enrichment analysis**: testing whether a gene list (say, genes up in tumors) contains more genes from a category than chance would predict, against a background list.
- **KEGG**: hand-drawn pathway maps (hsa04010 = human MAPK signaling) plus metabolism. Gene IDs look like hsa:7157. A **pathway** is a chain of molecules that together carry out a process (see Unit 5).
- **Reactome**: open, curated, hierarchical pathways (R-HSA-…). Often used with KEGG and MSigDB gene sets for enrichment.
- **STRING**: protein–protein **association** networks. It combines experiments, co-expression, text mining and genomic context into a confidence score (0–1, written 0–1000 in files). Edges are not always physical binding.
- **BioGRID / IntAct**: curated, experimentally observed interactions.

## Perturbation and chemistry

- **DepMap** (Cancer Dependency Map): genome-wide CRISPR knockout screens (see Unit 6) across 1,000+ **cancer cell lines**, which are tumor-derived cells grown indefinitely in the lab. Gene effect (Chronos) scores show which genes each line needs to survive.
- **ChEMBL**: bioactive molecules and their assay results (CHEMBL25 = aspirin). **PubChem**: chemical structures.
- **ENCODE** (Encyclopedia of DNA Elements): experiments mapping regulatory activity (ChIP-seq, ATAC/DNase, RNA-seq; see Unit 6) and candidate regulatory elements (cCREs). A main training source for sequence-to-function models.

## How you'll see this in a paper

- "Data are available at GEO under GSE123456": processed tables are there, and raw reads are linked via SRA (SRR runs).
- "Variants with gnomAD AF > 0.01 were treated as benign": a population-frequency filter.
- "Evaluated on ClinVar missense variants (≥1 star) not in the training set": standard variant-effect benchmarking; watch for leakage (see Unit 7).
- "GO enrichment (FDR < 0.05)": over-representation test with multiple-testing correction (FDR = false discovery rate).
- "Structures from AlphaFold DB where no PDB entry existed."
