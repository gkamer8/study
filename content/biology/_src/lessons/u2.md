### Key terms

- **Gene symbol**: the short official name of a gene, e.g. TP53.
- **Ortholog**: the corresponding gene in a different species (human TP53 ↔ mouse Trp53).
- **Paralog**: a related gene in the same species that arose by duplication (e.g. KRAS and NRAS).
- **Gene family**: a group of related genes named with a shared root plus numbers or letters.
- **Pseudogene**: a broken, non-functional copy of a gene.
- **Antisense RNA**: an RNA transcribed from the opposite strand of another gene.
- **Kilodalton (kDa)**: a unit of molecular mass equal to 1,000 Da; an average amino acid is ~0.11 of these.
- **Knockout**: an organism (or cell) in which a gene has been deliberately deleted or disabled.
- **Floxed**: describes a gene flanked by loxP sites, so it can be deleted wherever Cre is active.
- **Cre**: the recombinase enzyme that deletes DNA lying between two loxP sites.
- **HLA**: the highly variable human immune genes that display protein fragments to T cells.
- **Germline**: describes a variant that is inherited and present in every cell of the body.
- **Somatic**: describes a variant acquired in some cells during life, e.g. in a tumor.
- **Reference sequence**: the specific stored sequence record that variant coordinates are counted against.
- **Accession**: a database's stable identifier for a record.
- **Version**: the number after the dot in an accession, which increments when the stored sequence changes.
- **Assembly**: one released edition of a reference genome, e.g. GRCh38.
- **HGVS**: the standard notation for describing variants (c., p., g. prefixes).
- **rsID**: a dbSNP identifier for a known variant position.
- **Hotspot**: a position mutated unusually often in cancer.
- **Canonical isoform**: the transcript or protein version a database treats as the default.

## Why naming matters

Biology names one entity many ways. TP53, p53, *Trp53*, P04637, ENSG00000141510, Entrez Gene 7157 and HGNC:11998 all refer to the same human gene, its protein, or its mouse **ortholog** (the corresponding gene in another species). If you can parse names on sight you can join datasets, find the right record, and catch bugs such as mouse symbols in a human analysis or coordinates from the wrong genome version.

## Human gene symbols (HGNC)

The **HUGO Gene Nomenclature Committee (HGNC)** assigns each human gene one official **symbol**, a stable ID (TP53 = **HGNC:11998**), and a descriptive name ("tumor protein p53"). Symbols use only **uppercase letters and digits**, with no Greek letters. The gene for β-catenin is **CTNNB1**; the gene for TGF-β1 is **TGFB1**.

Conventions:
- **Gene in italics, protein in plain (roman) type**. *TP53* is the gene and TP53 (or p53) is its protein. Many papers are sloppy, but careful ones follow this.
- **Family names with numbers**. A **gene family** is a set of related genes, often created by duplication; members of the same species are **paralogs**. *CYP2D6* = cytochrome P450 family 2, subfamily D, member 6 (a drug-metabolizing enzyme). *SLC2A1* = solute carrier family 2 member 1, the GLUT1 glucose transporter. KRT (keratins), ZNF (zinc fingers), HOX, OR (olfactory receptors) and HLA follow the same pattern.
- **Placeholder names** for genes found before anyone knew what they do. *C9orf72* = "chromosome 9 open reading frame 72" (an ORF, or open reading frame, is a stretch that could encode protein; see Unit 1). *FAM*##A = "family with sequence similarity". *LINC*##### = long intergenic non-coding RNA, an RNA gene lying between protein-coding genes.
- **Suffixes**. **P** + number = **pseudogene**, a broken gene copy (*PTENP1*). **-AS1** = **antisense RNA**, transcribed from the opposite strand of the named gene. **MIR**## = a microRNA gene (*MIR21*); the mature microRNA is written hsa-miR-21-5p (hsa = *Homo sapiens*, 5p = from the 5' arm of its hairpin).
- **Symbols change**. In 2020 HGNC renamed genes that Excel silently converted to dates: SEPT1 → *SEPTIN1*, MARCH1 → *MARCHF1*. Old datasets still contain old symbols or mangled ones like "1-Sep". Map through previous symbols and aliases, or join on stable IDs instead.

**Protein names vs gene symbols.** Many proteins keep historical names. "p53" means a protein of ~53 **kDa** (kilodaltons, a mass unit) as it appeared on a gel. Some pairs to memorize: HER2 is the product of *ERBB2*, CD45 of *PTPRC*, Ki-67 of *MKI67*, GLUT1 of *SLC2A1*. One gene can encode more than one protein: *CDKN2A* makes both **p16^INK4a^** and **p14^ARF^**, using different first exons and different reading frames (see Unit 1).

## Other species

- **Mouse and rat**: only the first letter is capitalized, in italics (*Trp53*, *Kras*). Proteins are written in all caps (TRP53). Engineered **alleles** go in superscript. *Kras^LSL-G12D^* has a "lox-stop-lox" cassette blocking expression of a G12D mutant until an enzyme called **Cre** removes it. *Trp53^fl/fl^* means both copies are **floxed**: flanked by **loxP** sites, so the gene is deleted only in cells that express Cre. This is how mouse studies delete a gene in one tissue.
- **Yeast (*S. cerevisiae*)**: a normal (wild-type) gene is three uppercase letters plus a number in italics (*CDC28*). Lowercase (*cdc28*) is a recessive mutant allele. The protein is Cdc28 or Cdc28p. Systematic names encode location: **YFL039C** (*ACT1*) = Yeast, chromosome F (the 6th), Left arm, ORF 039, on the Crick strand (C; W = Watson; these are just yeast's names for the two DNA strands).
- **Zebrafish**: all lowercase italic (*tp53*). **Fruit fly (*Drosophila*)**: names often come from the mutant's appearance (*white*, *hedgehog*).

## Immune genes: HLA alleles

**HLA** genes encode proteins that display fragments of proteins to immune T cells. They are the most variable genes in the human genome, so they have their own allele naming. **HLA-DRB1\*15:01** reads: gene *HLA-DRB1*, then `*`, then **field 1 = allele group** (15), then **field 2 = the specific protein** (01). Optional field 3 records silent DNA differences inside the coding region, and field 4 records differences outside it (in introns or UTRs). A trailing N means a "null" allele that isn't expressed. "Two-field" (or "four-digit") typing means protein-level resolution.

## Variant notation

Some definitions first. A **variant** is any difference from the reference sequence. **Germline** variants are inherited and present in every cell; **somatic** variants arise in some cells, such as a tumor. A **hotspot** is a position mutated far more often than chance in cancer.

**rsIDs** (rs1042522) come from **dbSNP**, NCBI's variant catalog. An rsID names a *position plus the alleles seen there*, not one specific change, and it isn't tied to a genome version.

**HGVS** notation describes a change relative to a named **reference sequence**, which is a database record with an **accession** (a stable ID) and a **version** (the number after the dot, which increments when the sequence changes):

- `NM_000546.6:c.215C>G`: a **c.** (coding DNA) position on mRNA record NM_000546 version 6. **c.1 is the A of the ATG start codon.** c.-14 is 14 bases before it (in the 5' UTR); c.\*20 is 20 bases after the stop codon (in the 3' UTR). Intronic positions count from the nearest exon: c.88+1G>T is the first intron base after coding position 88, and c.89-2 is two bases before c.89.
- `NP_000537.3:p.Pro72Arg` (short form `p.P72R`): a **p.** (protein) change, proline to arginine at amino acid 72. Parentheses, as in `p.(Pro72Arg)`, mean predicted rather than measured. `p.Arg196Ter` or `p.Arg196*` = nonsense (Arg becomes stop). `p.Arg213ProfsTer8` = frameshift: Arg213 becomes Pro and the new frame hits a stop 8 codons later.
- `NC_000017.11:g.7676154G>C`: a **g.** (genomic) position on a chromosome record. The version (.11) pins the genome build.
- Also `n.` (non-coding RNA), `m.` (mitochondrial DNA, e.g. m.3243A>G), and `r.` (RNA).
- Indels: `c.5266dupC` (duplication), `c.68_69delAG` (deletion), `delins` (deletion-insertion).

Those three examples are one variant written three ways: TP53 c.215C>G = p.Pro72Arg = rs1042522, a common harmless polymorphism. The genomic change reads G>C rather than C>G because TP53 lies on the minus strand. Cancer hotspots are usually quoted at the protein level: **BRAF V600E** (c.1799T>A), **KRAS G12D** (c.35G>A), **EGFR L858R**.

## Identifier formats you must recognize

The databases themselves are covered in Unit 3; here is how to recognize their IDs.

| Pattern | Source | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ENSG00000141510 | Ensembl | gene (ENST = transcript, ENSP = protein, ENSMUSG = mouse gene). A `.N` suffix is the version |
| NM_000546.6 | RefSeq (NCBI) | curated mRNA. NR_ = curated non-coding RNA; XM_/XR_ = computationally predicted |
| NP_000537.3 | RefSeq | curated protein (XP_ = predicted) |
| NC_000017.11 | RefSeq | complete chromosome 17 (.11 = GRCh38, .10 = GRCh37). NG_ = a genomic region |
| P04637, A0A024R1R8 | UniProt | protein accession, 6 or 10 characters. P53_HUMAN is the human-readable entry *name* |
| P04637-2 | UniProt | isoform 2 of that protein |
| 7157 | NCBI Gene (Entrez) | numeric gene ID |
| HGNC:11998 | HGNC | gene ID |
| 1TUP | PDB | experimental 3D structure: 4 characters, first is a digit |
| AF-P04637-F1 | AlphaFold DB | predicted structure for UniProt P04637, fragment 1 |
| rs1042522 | dbSNP | variant |
| 191170 | OMIM | 6-digit entry number for a gene or genetic disease |

Within UniProt, **Swiss-Prot** entries are reviewed by curators and **TrEMBL** entries are automatic and unreviewed (the vast majority). **UniRef50/90/100** cluster all UniProt sequences at 50/90/100% identity and are the usual training sets for protein language models.

## How you'll see this in a paper

- "Ensembl IDs were mapped to HGNC symbols with biomaRt": joining annotation sources, usually after stripping version suffixes.
- "MARCH1" or "1-Mar" in a supplementary table: a pre-2020 or Excel-mangled gene list.
- "R175H", "R248Q", "R273H": TP53 hotspot missense mutations in one-letter protein shorthand.
- "Trained on UniRef50 (release 2021_03)": UniProt clustered at 50% identity.
- "NM_000059.4:c.5946delT": a BRCA2 frameshift on a specific transcript version. Exon numbering can change between versions, so the version matters.
